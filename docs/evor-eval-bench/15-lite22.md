# lite22 — the first within-run measurement of the flip rate

144 runs (n=4), 94 minutes, **$105.83**. Paired against lite19, never against lite14
(different instrument — see doc 13).

## The numbers, and what survives scrutiny

| paired, scored in both | lite19 | lite22 |
|---|---|---|
| ALL (n=51) | 74.4 | **80.0** |
| non-tick (n=36) | 89.7 | **89.7** |
| tick (n=15) | 42.9 | **62.5** |

**The tick gain is the five fix-sets landing**: the capability-gap trap, the pointer
contradiction, the `kind` registry correction, the L4 severity floor, and the
per-call denial flag. TP 3→5, FN 5→3 on fifteen positions.

**The identical non-tick figure is cancellation, not stability.** 9 of 36 non-tick
positions changed and the totals landed in the same place. This is the third run
where that has happened and the third time it needs saying: an unchanged F1 over
changed cells is not evidence of a stable instrument.

Overall, 17 of 51 paired positions changed (33%) — squarely at the replication floor,
so **the +5.6 on ALL cannot be attributed to the fixes from this comparison alone.**
The tick's +19.6 is large enough to be worth more than the floor, and it is supported
by the mechanism traces rather than by the delta.

## The measurement that did not need two runs

n=4 makes the flip rate observable *within a single run*. Of 31 cells with more than
one scored record:

```
unanimous   19
SPLIT       12   (39%)
```

```
A3-critic-gate-codes-pos                 TP,TP,FN
E2-tick-pointer-unproducible-neg         TN,TN,FP
E3-evaluation-did-not-complete-pos       TP,FN,FN
L2-active-run-job-disagree-neg           TN,FP,FP,FP
L2-active-run-job-disagree-pos           FN,FN,TP,FN
L4-first-job-failed-second-succeeded-neg TN,FP,FP,FP
T1-blob-vs-artifact-control              PARTIAL,FAIL,PASS
T1-blob-vs-artifact-target               PARTIAL,FAIL,PARTIAL
T2-artifact-slot-control                 PASS,FAIL,FAIL,FAIL
T2-artifact-slot-target                  FAIL,PASS,PASS,FAIL
T4-enforcement-zone-neg                  TN,FP,TN,FP
T5-no-slot-pos                           FN,TP,TP,TP
```

This is the cheapest determinism instrument available and it should have been the
first thing run: **one run at n=4 measures the flip rate directly**, where the
replication study needed two full runs to estimate it.

It also gives a modal verdict per cell for free. Nine of the twelve splits have a
clear majority (3-1 or 3-0); three are genuinely 2-2 (`T2-artifact-slot-target`,
`T4-enforcement-zone-neg`, and `T1-blob-vs-artifact-target` across three tiers).

## Two cells the split table indicts

`T2-artifact-slot-*` splits in **both** directions — control 1/4 PASS, target 2/4
PASS. The traced cause is a sentence-initial capital in the prompt (`"Run-live-01."`)
that the agent copies into `run_id`, missing the state read and defaulting to tick 1.
That was a 16/16 clean discriminator in earlier runs; at n=4 it now splits, which is
consistent with the same coin being flipped rather than a different mechanism.

`L2-active-run-job-disagree-neg` and `L4-...-neg` are both `TN,FP,FP,FP` — the
negative halves firing on three of four draws. Under the silence-masking asymmetry
(doc 10) a negative half is scored on whether the agent stayed quiet, so a run where
it happened to say more scores FP. These two are the asymmetry, visible.

## Voids

31 of 144 (21.5%), of which 22 are `denied_undeclared`. The governor-subtraction
wiring (`1f3d2c1`) landed **after** lite22 launched, so those 22 are expected and
should fall in the next run. Four `no terminal JSON answer` voids on
`L3-terminal-job-nonadvancing-tick-pos` are a separate, still-open defect: the JSON
status line is a contract the bench asserts and never enforces.

## Cost

$105.83 at n=4 against ~$48 at n=2. The n=4 split table is worth the premium once;
whether it is worth it every iteration depends on which of doc 14's three
reformulations is adopted.
